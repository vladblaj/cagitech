02 / FROM IMAGE TO EVENT

Keep the picture local. Use the event.

The value is not another camera screen. It is a consistent record of visits, occupancy, and exceptions that can be reviewed by an operator.

⌑

Observe

A fixed view covers the yard approach and one or more loading docks. A camera or recorded clip supplies frames to the processor.

◇

Track

Computer vision finds trucks and people, keeps short-lived track IDs, and checks their positions against drawn zones and crossing lines.

▤

Understand

Rules turn those movements into arrival, dock entry, activity, idle, and exit events. The dashboard shows timings and lets you export visits.

Today: laptop prototype tested with simulated sequences and public recorded video. Before a warehouse pilot: validate camera angles, detection accuracy, and any custom forklift model.

03 / A PRACTICAL PILOT

Start with one camera and one dock.

The proposed installation uses your existing fixed camera where compatible. The pilot is a small, reversible setup: no warehouse-system integration is required to learn whether the view is useful.

1

Check the view

Choose a camera that clearly sees the truck approach, the dock threshold, and the loading area. Confirm image quality and permission to use the feed.

2

Connect the processor

Connect an on-site computer to power and the camera network. RTSP/IP-camera compatibility and edge hardware are verified during the pilot.

3

Mark the zones

Draw the yard and dock areas and a loading line on a sample frame. Set thresholds for brief occlusions and idle time.

4

Compare with reality

Run a short supervised trial, compare events with observed activity, adjust zones, then decide whether to expand to more cameras.

What you need for a first trial

One suitable fixed camera or a sample recording, local network access, a laptop or edge computer, and an operator who can verify the resulting timeline.

04 / PRIVACY BY DESIGN

Process video. Do not build a video archive.

The intended on-site setup analyzes live frames locally and does not save a continuous recording by default. What remains is a small operational event record: time, zone, track ID, and duration.

01

Camera frames

Processed on site in the proposed live setup. No continuous recording or cloud video upload is part of the default design.

02

Offline imports

The laptop prototype temporarily stores an imported video on the laptop while processing, then deletes that import when processing finishes or is cancelled.

03

Event history

Visit times and event metadata are retained locally for review and CSV export. Retention periods, access, and any optional review clips are decided with the pilot site.

What the prototype proves — and what it doesn't.

A useful pilot starts with honest boundaries.

Working today

A local dashboard, configurable zones, truck and person tracking on recorded video, visit timelines, occupancy estimates, movement trails, and CSV export.

Needs site validation

Live camera integration, reliable forklift detection, pallet counts, camera-specific accuracy, multi-camera identity, and deployment on a dedicated edge device.
